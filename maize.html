The Maize Manifesto

I'm a big fan of the show Clarkson's Farm. I initially watched it because the original three had left Top Gear, and I wanted more Clarkson content.

In the first season, Clarkson realizes that beetles have eaten almost ten acres of one of his fields, costing him about £4,000. Obviously it made for great TV, but farmers go through this shit every year. The stuff that happens is very real, and farmers suffer because of it. Weather, disease, pests: all things that make a farmer's life hell.

I've talked to many farmers while working on Maize, and for many of them every day is a dice roll. They have very little control over the things that might destroy a field (and with it an entire year of profits), and those things just pop up randomly. It's truly devastating when it does happen.

And the biggest thing is that it's not like farmers can't solve these problems. I know many farmers who have been at it their entire lives. They know exactly what to do when things come up. But with 200 acres of farmland, the problem isn't how to fix a problem. It's where the problem is.

My co-founders and I recently visited one of our partner farms, run by a dude called Fred. He lost a 30-acre field to armyworms over the growing season because it took him three weeks to find the issue.

Big tech companies do have solutions: $30k all-in-one pesticide, irrigation, monitoring, high-fidelity... all the buzzwords they can use. But small farmers can't give a shit about these drones when the cost of one would eat their entire profit margin for the year. And why would they need something to go out and water plants for them? They've been doing it their entire lives. Sure, these all-in-one drones might work for larger industrial farms (and I fully agree this is the future of industrial farming), but they don't work for small farms, and small farms are being left behind because of it. It pisses me off.

This isn't really even meant to sell you on the idea of Maize, just to paint a picture of what these farmers are going through. Everyone says that tech and automation are the future of farming. The future of what farming? Large farms? Because family farms are not benefiting from these innovations at all right now. The gap will only get larger as time goes on. In 30 years, we'll lose all of these generational small farms, and crop production will become centralized in a few big corporations.

So to put it simply: small farms are blocked out of precision agriculture right now, and Maize is an attempt to bring this technology to them.
